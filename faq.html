Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about Coast FIRE, the calculators, and your path to financial independence.

Why use this FAQ?

We've compiled the most common questions from our community. Use these answers to understand Coast FIRE better before diving into the calculators.

Coast FIRE Fundamentals

What exactly is Coast FIRE?

Coast FIRE is a retirement strategy where you save aggressively now, then let compound growth carry you to traditional retirement age without additional contributions. Once you reach your Coast FIRE number, the market does the rest.

Key insight:

The goal is to stop saving aggressively while you still have decades for compounding to work its magic. This is different from traditional FIRE because you don't need to accumulate enough assets immediately to live off entirely—you can work part-time or in a career you enjoy.

How does Coast FIRE differ from traditional FIRE?

Traditional FIRE: Save enough for 25-30x expenses before retiring. Stop working entirely.

Coast FIRE: Build enough assets that compound growth alone will reach your full FIRE number by age 65 (or whenever you choose to retire). Once there, stop saving and let investments grow.

The difference:

Traditional FIRE gets you to the finish line fast but requires high savings rates. Coast FIRE extends the journey slightly but reduces pressure once you hit a milestone.

At what age should I aim for Coast FIRE?

This depends on your current financial situation. Most people reach Coast FIRE between ages 35-45, depending on:

  • Current savings and assets
  • Your savings rate (percentage of income saved)
  • Expected investment returns (historically ~7% after inflation)
  • Target retirement age

Rule of thumb:

If you're in your 20s or early 30s with a reasonable savings rate (15-20% of income), you could hit Coast FIRE by late 30s. If starting later or with lower savings rates, it might be mid-to-late 40s.

Can I restart saving after coasting?

Absolutely! Coast FIRE doesn't mean you can never save again. Once you reach your Coast FIRE number, you have flexibility:

  • Stop saving entirely and let compounding work
  • Save occasionally in good markets
  • Restart aggressively if market conditions improve

The key insight:

Coast FIRE gives you optionality—you're not stuck. You can work because you want to, not because you have to.

How to Use the Calculators

What inputs are required for the Coast FIRE calculator?
  • Current Age: How old you are now
  • Target Retirement Age: When you plan to stop working (typically 65-70)
  • Annual Expenses: What you expect to spend in retirement (in today's dollars)
  • Current Savings: Total invested assets (401k, IRA, brokerage accounts)
  • Expected Annual Return: Historical average is 7% for diversified stock portfolios
  • Safe Withdrawal Rate: Typically 4% based on the Trinity Study
How do I enter Social Security benefits?

Our calculators use a conservative approach: they assume no Social Security income to give you the most realistic timeline.

Why this matters:

If you expect Social Security to cover 30-50% of your expenses, enter that as a percentage reduction. For example, if $50,000 annual expenses include $20,000 from Social Security, enter $30,000 instead.

Use ssa.gov/my-account to check your estimated benefits before finalizing retirement plans.

What if my expected return is lower than 7%?

A 7% return represents historical averages, not guarantees. If you're concerned about volatility or want a more conservative estimate:

  • 6%: Still reasonable for a balanced portfolio (60-40 stocks/bonds)
  • 5%: Conservative stock-heavy or target-date funds
  • 4%: Very conservative allocation

Important note:

Your calculator results will vary year-to-year. Use the conservative return to build confidence, but don't be afraid to use historical averages for planning. The key is maintaining your allocation during market downturns.

Can I adjust the withdrawal rate?

Yes! Use the slider to adjust from 3% to 10%:

  • 4-5%: Traditional safe withdrawal rates (Trinity Study)
  • 6-7%: More aggressive, higher risk of portfolio depletion
  • 3%: Very conservative, supports longer retirement

Why it matters:

The withdrawal rate directly impacts your Coast FIRE number. A lower rate means you need more assets to coast, but also extends your portfolio's lifespan. Use your personal risk tolerance as a guide.

Understanding Your Results

What does my Coast FIRE number mean?

This is the exact amount you need invested TODAY to reach your full FIRE goal by traditional retirement age without saving another dollar.

Example:

If your Coast FIRE number is $250,000 and you currently have $150,000 in savings, you need to save another $100,000 through compound growth. If you hit that target at age 38, you can coast until age 65.

Already there?

If the calculator shows you've already reached Coast FIRE (indicated in green), congratulations! You can stop saving aggressively now and let compounding work its magic.

How do I interpret "years to coast"?

This tells you when you'll stop needing to save aggressively. If the calculator shows 7 years to coast from age 35, that means by age 42, your investments will have grown enough that compounding alone can get you to retirement.

What happens after hitting Coast FIRE:

  • Market growth continues without additional savings
  • You can reduce work hours or switch careers
  • Your portfolio becomes truly passive income
What is the "gap" number?

The gap is how much you need to save before coasting begins. If your Coast FIRE number is $200,000 and you have $100,000 saved, your gap is $100,000.

Why close the gap:

The smaller your gap, the sooner you can coast. Consider:

  • Increasing savings rate temporarily
  • Selling a property or other asset
  • Career move to higher income (if age allows)
Does the calculator account for inflation?

Yes. The calculator assumes:

  • Inputs are in today's dollars
  • Expected return of 7% is after-inflation (real returns)
  • Your expenses grow with inflation over time

Important note:

The calculator shows results in today's dollars for simplicity. Your future portfolio will naturally keep pace with inflation through nominal returns exceeding inflation.

Investment Strategies

What portfolio allocation should I use?

Coast FIRE works with any portfolio that maintains long-term compounding:

  • Growth-oriented (recommended): 90-100% stocks
  • Balanced: 70-80% stocks / 20-30% bonds
  • Conservative: 50-60% stocks / 40-50% bonds

Key principle:

During the coasting phase, you have decades where market downturns can recover. Avoid locking into overly conservative portfolios during this time.

Should I use a target-date fund?

Yes! Target-date funds automatically adjust allocation as you approach retirement:

  • Example: If coasting at age 40 targeting age 65, use target date fund for year 2040
  • The fund becomes more conservative automatically as you approach retirement

Benefits:

Automatic rebalancing, professional management, tax-efficient structure. Ideal for hands-off investors.

How often should I check my progress?

At minimum, review annually:

  • Portfolio value vs. Coast FIRE target
  • Market conditions (are we in bull or bear market?)
  • Changes in expenses or life circumstances

Important mindset:

Don't over-check during short-term market volatility. Remember that compound growth works best with a long-term perspective. Coast FIRE is about trusting the process.

Social Security & Taxes

How do Social Security benefits factor into planning?

Use our calculator with conservative assumptions, then adjust for expected benefits:

  1. Enter full expenses for baseline planning
  2. Check ssa.gov/my-account for your estimated benefit
  3. If SS covers ~40% of expenses, reduce your target by that amount

Example:

With $50,000 annual expenses and SS providing $20,000, you only need your portfolio to cover $30,000. This significantly reduces your Coast FIRE number.

When should I claim Social Security?

This depends on your specific situation:

  • Age 62: Early claiming (permanently reduced benefit)
  • Full Retirement Age: Typically 66-67 depending on birth year
  • Age 70: Delayed claiming (maximum benefit)

Coast FIRE strategy:

Consider delaying SS claiming while you coast. You can work part-time to cover living expenses without claiming benefits. When you finally hit full Coast FIRE, continue working and delay SS until age 70 for maximum benefit.

What about taxes on retirement income?

Taxes vary based on:

  • State of residence (some have no income tax)
  • Total portfolio value
  • Income from other sources

Planning tips:

  • Consider geographic diversification (low-tax states)
  • Roth conversions can fill tax brackets efficiently
  • Invest in low-tax asset classes during high-income years

Common Scenarios

I'm 28, earning $75k, saving $15k/year. When can I coast?

This depends on your expenses and investment returns. With a typical savings rate of ~20% and 7% annual returns, you could reach Coast FIRE by age 35-38.

What to optimize:

  • Increase savings rate to 25-30% if possible
  • Maximize employer matching on retirement accounts
  • Avoid lifestyle creep as income increases
I'm 45 with $200k in investments. Is that enough to coast?

It depends on your expenses and target retirement age. If you plan to retire at 65 (20 years from now) and have annual expenses of $40,000:

  • Your Full FIRE Target = $40,000 / 0.04 = $1,000,000
  • Coast FIRE Number = $1,000,000 / 1.07^20 = ~$329,000

You currently have $200k, so you need to save until your portfolio reaches ~$330k. That could take 5-7 more years depending on savings rate and returns.

I lost my job last year. Does Coast FIRE still apply?

Absolutely! In fact, it may be more attractive now:

  • You have flexibility to work part-time or find passion projects
  • No employer benefits tied to employment
  • Market downturns might create buying opportunities

Opportunity perspective:

Coast FIRE gives you optionality—you're not desperate for employment. This is a strength, not a vulnerability.

Can I achieve Coast FIRE and then move abroad?

Yes! This is one of Coast FIRE's unique advantages. You can:

  1. Achieve Coast FIRE in a high-cost area
  2. Migrate to lower-cost location (Thailand, Portugal, Costa Rica)
  3. Your portfolio continues compounding regardless of location

Example:

Coast FIRE at age 45 with $330k in a high-cost area. Move to Thailand where your purchasing power doubles or triples. Your portfolio continues growing toward retirement target regardless of location changes.

What if the market crashes during my coasting phase?

Remember: Coast FIRE works because compound growth is a long-term phenomenon:

  • Market downturns are temporary (decades-long view)
  • Historical average returns still hold over time
  • No need to add more money—you have no forced selling pressure

The advantage of coasting:

Unlike traditional retirement where you're pulling money out, during Coast FIRE you're letting time work for you. A market downturn actually benefits you—it reduces your portfolio value but also means lower starting point when compounding resumes.

How do I handle lifestyle inflation as income increases?

Be intentional about saving rate vs. spending:

  • Maintain modest lifestyle: Don't upgrade car/house with every raise
  • Automate savings: Set up automatic transfers to investment accounts
  • Live below means: Use percentage-of-income budget (e.g., 50/30/20 rule)

Coast FIRE timing:

The earlier you resist lifestyle inflation, the sooner you can coast. Every $1k saved at age 30 is worth ~$15k in today's dollars by age 60 due to compounding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to all your Coast FIRE questions
What is Coast FIRE?

Coast FIRE means saving aggressively until compound interest alone can grow your portfolio to your full FIRE number by traditional retirement age.

How much do I need to coast?

You need enough invested assets such that, at retirement age (say 65), they will equal your "FIRE number" without any more contributions. That depends on your spending, expected returns (typically 7-8%), and withdrawal rate assumption (commonly 4%).

What's the "safe withdrawal rate"?

The 4% rule means withdrawing 4% of your portfolio annually, adjusted for inflation. For Coast FIRE, we assume:

  • Conservative: 3-3.5%
  • Moderate: 4%
  • Aggressive: 5%
How does Coast FIRE differ from traditional FIRE?

Traditional FIRE: Save aggressively all the way to retirement.

Coast FIRE: Save aggressively in early career, then stop or reduce savings by mid-career while compound growth carries you to your target.

When can I coast?

Coast eligibility:

  • Portfolio / FIRE Number ≥ 65% and ≤ 100%
  • Your portfolio is on track to reach full FIRE number by retirement age
How does the calculator work?

The calculator asks for current assets, annual expenses, expected return (7%), and withdrawal rate (4%). It projects your FIRE target and when you can stop saving.

What if I'm over 60?

If you're over 60, traditional FIRE is often better because you have fewer years for compounding. However, Coast FIRE can still work if you've already accumulated a large enough portfolio.

How do I account for inflation?

All numbers in our calculations are in nominal (today's) dollars. We use pre-inflation figures:

  • Your Annual Expenses input is your current spending power
  • 7-8% returns are historical nominal averages (which already include inflation)
  • 4% withdrawal rate is a traditional rule of thumb for portfolio sustainability

Over a 30-35 year horizon, a 7% nominal return portfolio typically outpaces average inflation, allowing your purchasing power to grow even if you don't save another dollar. That's the Coast FIRE advantage.

Can I use a higher withdrawal rate?

Yes! Higher withdrawal rates reduce your FIRE number, but increase sequence-of-returns risk. The calculator lets you pick your own rate.

What about Social Security?

You can claim at age 62 (reduced), full retirement age (typically 66-67), or delay to 70 for maximum benefit. In Coast FIRE, you may coast on your portfolio for years before claiming Social Security.

What investment allocation should I use?

Ages 20-40: 90-100% equities (time in market is your friend)

Ages 40-55: 70-80% equities, 20-30% bonds

Ages 55+: 60-70% equities, 30-40% bonds (preservation becomes more important)
